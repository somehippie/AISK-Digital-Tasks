# Contributing

Thanks for considering a contribution to the AI Starter Kit.

This is a research-grounded curriculum, not a wiki — most sections exist because of a specific cited finding, and changes should engage with that reasoning rather than just personal preference. Part 1 and Part 8 of [`docs/ai-starter-kit.md`](docs/ai-starter-kit.md) explain a lot of decisions that can look arbitrary until you've read the rationale behind them.

## Before you open a pull request

For anything beyond a typo or a broken link, **open an issue first** describing:

- What you think is missing, wrong, or could be improved
- Why — ideally with a source, if you're proposing a factual or research-based change
- Whether it changes a session's structure, timing, or sequencing (these are usually the most deliberate design choices in the document)

This avoids a large PR being declined after the fact because it conflicts with the underlying design logic.

## If you don't use a terminal

Most of the people this curriculum is written for do not use a command line, and facilitators correcting a fact in their own material should not have to learn one first.

You do not need a terminal to contribute here. Two routes, neither requiring any software beyond a browser:

- **Edit in the browser.** Open any file on GitHub, click the pencil icon, make your change, and GitHub will create the branch and open the pull request for you. This is the shortest path for a typo, a broken link, or a corrected figure.
- **[GitHub Desktop](https://desktop.github.com/).** A free graphical app for Mac and Windows that clones, branches, commits and opens pull requests with buttons instead of commands. Use this if you are changing several files at once, or want to read your changes side by side before submitting.

If neither appeals, open an issue describing the change in plain language and someone will make the edit. A correction reported badly is far more useful than a correction not reported.

## Kinds of contributions especially welcome

- **Pilot feedback.** If you've run any part of this curriculum with a real learner or group, that field evidence is the most valuable kind of contribution this project can receive.
- **Source updates.** Several cited figures (energy/water-per-query estimates especially) are explicitly flagged in the document as having a short shelf life. Corrections with a dated, credible source are welcome.
- **Accessibility improvements.** Suggestions that make the material more usable for facilitators working with learners who have low digital literacy, limited vision, or other accessibility needs.
- **Typo and clarity fixes.** Always welcome, no issue needed first.

## What's likely to be declined

- Removing the human-first sequencing, the honest ledger, or the verification-before-capability ordering. These are the load-bearing design decisions of the whole kit and are backed by cited research in Part 1.
- Adding vendor-specific tool recommendations by brand name in the core curriculum. Part 6 deliberately organizes tools by job-to-be-done because brand-specific advice goes stale within a quarter — a link to a maintained tool list is preferred over hardcoding brand names into the lesson plan.
- Scope creep toward a general AI/ML technical course (bias, model architecture, etc.). That's a different, valuable curriculum, but it's not this one — see "Where this kit sits relative to other programs" in the document for why that's a deliberate boundary.

## Style

- Markdown, GitHub-flavored, with tables used the way the rest of the document uses them (structured comparisons, not decoration).
- Cite sources inline the way the existing document does, and add full citations to the Source Notes section at the end.
- Keep the same voice: direct, evidence-first, willing to say "we don't know" rather than smoothing over an open question.
